# Miro Workshop Facilitation Guide

Practical guidance for running collaborative service blueprint workshops in Miro based on Marc Stickdorn's workshop methodologies.

## Pre-Workshop Preparation (Critical Phase)

**Time investment**: Spend 2-3x the workshop duration on preparation

### 1. Research Foundation

Before the workshop, gather:
- Customer interview transcripts
- User observation notes
- Support ticket summaries
- Analytics data (heatmaps, drop-off points)
- Existing journey maps or process documents

### 2. Pre-Populate the Miro Board

**Don't start with blank canvas**. Pre-populate with:
- Draft persona(s) in corner of board
- Suggested stages across the top
- Known customer actions in grey text (to be validated)
- Known pain points flagged with 🔴
- System names in support lanes

**Why**: Participants validate/correct rather than create from scratch (faster, more focused)

### 3. Board Setup Checklist

- [ ] Create clear swim lanes with labels
- [ ] Add stage dividers across the top
- [ ] Include legend explaining icons/colours
- [ ] Add timer widget (set to odd numbers: 7, 16, 19 min)
- [ ] Create breakout frames for group work
- [ ] Test all templates and sticky note colours
- [ ] Pre-load participant avatars/names
- [ ] Set up voting dots for prioritisation
- [ ] Include "parking lot" frame for off-topic items

### 4. Participant Selection

Invite representatives from:
- **Customer-facing roles** (sales, support, account managers)
- **Operations** (fulfilment, logistics, operations)
- **Technical teams** (developers, architects, DevOps)
- **Business stakeholders** (product, marketing, finance)
- **Actual customers/users** (if possible - high value)

**Optimal size**: 8-12 people. Fewer than 6 lacks diversity; more than 15 becomes unwieldy.

## Workshop Structure (Example: 3-Hour Session)

### Part 1: Warm-up and Context (20 min)

**Purpose**: Build energy, create psychological safety, establish shared understanding

#### Warm-up Activity (7 min)
Use "Yes, and..." improv exercise:
1. Pair people up
2. One person makes a statement
3. Partner responds "Yes, and..." (adding to the idea)
4. Continue for 2 minutes, then switch roles

**Why**: Breaks down hierarchies, creates accepting atmosphere, gets people comfortable building on ideas

#### Context Setting (13 min)
- Introduce the blueprint methodology briefly (5 min)
- Share the persona we're mapping (3 min)
- Present key research findings (5 min)

### Part 2: Map Customer Actions (45 min)

**Mode**: Parallel contribution (many pens, one page)

#### Activity 1: Identify Stages (7 min)
- Each person individually adds stage suggestions on sticky notes
- Group review and consolidation
- Vote on final 5-7 stages

#### Activity 2: Map Customer Actions (19 min)
- Assign 2-3 people per stage
- Each group adds customer actions for their stage
- Use yellow sticky notes
- Aim for 3-5 actions per stage

**Facilitator prompts**:
- "What does the customer DO first?"
- "What happens next?"
- "Don't forget waiting time!"
- "Be specific - use verbs"

#### Activity 3: Add Emotional Journey (16 min)
- Each group adds emotion indicators above their actions
- Use emoji sticky notes or draw sentiment curve
- Share out: which moments are pain points?

**Break** (10 min)

### Part 3: Build Frontstage and Backstage (50 min)

**Mode**: Sequential contribution (one pen, one page) - facilitator drives

#### Activity 4: Frontstage Mapping (16 min)
- Start at beginning of journey
- For each customer action, ask: "What visible interaction enables this?"
- Add to Frontstage lane
- Distinguish human vs computer interactions
- Use different sticky note colours

#### Activity 5: Backstage Mapping (19 min)
- For each frontstage action, ask: "What behind-the-scenes work enables this?"
- Add to appropriate backstage role lanes
- Create new lanes as needed
- Flag handoff points between roles

#### Activity 6: Support Systems (16 min)
- For each backstage action, ask: "What systems, data, or infrastructure enable this?"
- Add to support lanes
- Include ERP, databases, third-party services
- Draw connections/dependencies

### Part 4: Identify Opportunities (40 min)

**Mode**: Individual work then share (many pens, many pages → consolidation)

#### Activity 7: Pain Points Deep Dive (16 min)
- Review emotional low points
- Each person adds sticky notes: "What causes this pain?"
- Group and theme pain points
- Vote on top 3 most critical

#### Activity 8: Opportunity Brainstorming (19 min)
- Use "How Might We?" framing
- For top 3 pain points, brainstorm solutions
- Each person generates ideas on sticky notes (7 min silent generation)
- Share and cluster (12 min)

**Example HMW prompts**:
- "How might we reduce waiting time during checkout?"
- "How might we enable multi-user collaboration?"
- "How might we provide real-time status visibility?"

#### Activity 9: Opportunity Prioritisation (7 min)
- Vote on opportunities (3 votes per person)
- Identify top 2-3 to carry forward

### Part 5: Wrap-up and Next Steps (15 min)

- **Key insights summary** (5 min): Facilitator synthesises main findings
- **Next steps** (5 min): Who owns what follow-up actions
- **Feedback** (5 min): Quick temperature check on workshop

## Facilitation Techniques

### Liquid Timing
Use precise, odd timings:
- 7 minutes (not 5)
- 16 minutes (not 15)
- 19 minutes (not 20)

**Why**: Rounded numbers make people relax and procrastinate. Odd numbers create urgency.

**Hide the timer**: Don't let participants see it. Announce "2 minutes left" rather than showing countdown.

### Managing Energy: Dramatic Arc

Design energy flow like "Boom-Wow-Wow-Wow-Boom":
- Start high (warm-up)
- Build momentum (customer mapping)
- Peak (opportunity brainstorming)
- Sustain (prioritisation)
- End high (commitments to action)

**Avoid**: Flat energy throughout or front-loading all energy at start.

### Group Size Variations

**Divergent thinking** (generating ideas):
- Use individual work or pairs
- Many sticky notes, many voices
- Short time boxes (7-12 min)

**Convergent thinking** (consolidating, deciding):
- Use full group discussion
- Facilitator-led or voting
- Moderate time boxes (12-19 min)

### Handling Common Challenges

#### Challenge: Participants focus only on backstage operations
**Solution**: Constantly redirect to customer experience. Ask "What is the customer doing while this happens?"

#### Challenge: Scope creep - too many stages/actions
**Solution**: Use parking lot for out-of-scope items. Remind of 20-25 action target.

#### Challenge: Disagreement about process flow
**Solution**: Mark as question/assumption. Plan validation activity with real users post-workshop.

#### Challenge: Silent participants not contributing
**Solution**: Use individual sticky note activities. Call on people directly: "What do you see from your team's perspective?"

#### Challenge: Dominant voices drowning others
**Solution**: Use "silent generation" phases (7 min individual work, no talking). Rotate who presents each section.

## Miro-Specific Tips

### Board Navigation
- **Frames**: Use frames to create "rooms" within board (research corner, blueprint area, opportunity zone)
- **Mini-map**: Keep mini-map visible so people understand where they are
- **Zoom presets**: Create saved views for key areas (Ctrl/Cmd + 1-9)

### Sticky Note Management
- **Colour coding**: Consistent colours for each lane or type
  - Yellow: Customer actions
  - Orange: Emotional indicators
  - Blue: Frontstage
  - Green: Backstage
  - Pink: Systems
  - Red: Pain points
  - Purple: Opportunities
- **Size matters**: Use large sticky notes (don't constrain thinking)
- **Text size**: Ensure readable at 100% zoom (14pt+ recommended)

### Collaboration Features
- **Cursors visible**: Keep all cursors showing so people see activity
- **Timer widget**: Set and display centrally during timed activities
- **Voting**: Use built-in voting feature for prioritisation
- **Comments**: Encourage use of comments for questions/clarifications

### Templates to Prepare
1. **Persona card template**: Photo, name, quote, goals, frustrations
2. **Stage column template**: Pre-sized columns with divider lines
3. **Pain point template**: Red icon + description box
4. **Opportunity template**: Light bulb + "How Might We?" prompt
5. **Voting template**: Dots or stars for prioritisation

## Remote vs In-Person Considerations

### Remote-Specific Adaptations
- **Shorter sessions**: 2-2.5 hours instead of 3+ hours (attention span)
- **More breaks**: Every 45-60 min instead of 90 min
- **Pre-work**: Send prep materials 3 days before (persona, research summary)
- **Backup facilitator**: Have co-facilitator managing chat, technical issues
- **Ice breaker**: Start with camera-on personal check-in (1 word to describe mood)

### In-Person with Miro
- **Projection**: Display Miro board on large screen
- **Hybrid input**: Some people use laptops, others call out to facilitator
- **Physical sticky notes**: Transfer to Miro afterward (or take photos during)
- **Simultaneous physical board**: Keep both formats if group prefers tactile

## Post-Workshop Actions

### Within 24 Hours
- [ ] Clean up the Miro board (align sticky notes, clarify text)
- [ ] Add photos of participants to frame corners (acknowledgment)
- [ ] Synthesise key insights into 1-page summary
- [ ] Capture action items with owners and dates
- [ ] Send thank-you email with board link and summary

### Within 1 Week
- [ ] Share polished version of blueprint
- [ ] Schedule follow-up session to validate with users
- [ ] Create hierarchy of related blueprints (zoom levels)
- [ ] Link blueprint to project roadmap/backlog

### Ongoing
- [ ] Set up quarterly review cycle
- [ ] Assign blueprint ownership
- [ ] Track opportunities → implemented features
- [ ] Update blueprint as service evolves

## Quality Checklist for Final Blueprint

Before considering workshop complete, verify:

**Completeness**:
- [ ] All swimlanes populated
- [ ] Stages clearly defined
- [ ] Emotional journey included
- [ ] Pain points documented
- [ ] Opportunities identified

**Accuracy**:
- [ ] Based on real research, not assumptions
- [ ] Validated by customer-facing teams
- [ ] Technical feasibility confirmed by dev team
- [ ] Timeline/sequence makes sense

**Actionability**:
- [ ] Pain points are specific, not vague
- [ ] Opportunities are achievable, not fantasies
- [ ] Top priorities clearly marked
- [ ] Next steps defined with owners

**Usability**:
- [ ] Legend included explaining icons/colours
- [ ] Text large enough to read at 100% zoom
- [ ] Board organised logically (left to right flow)
- [ ] Link to related materials (research, personas, other blueprints)

## Stickdorn's 10 Workshop Tips (Summary)

1. **Spend significant time on preparation** (2-3x workshop duration)
2. **Warm up properly** - get people to laugh together
3. **Always bring enough materials** (extra sticky notes, templates)
4. **Vary group sizes strategically** (individual → pairs → full group)
5. **Manage time by splitting challenges** (use liquid timing technique)
6. **Use facilitation tricks** (odd timings, hidden timers, parking lots)
7. **Be extremely specific with instructions** (show examples, not just describe)
8. **Use large templates** - don't constrain thinking with small spaces
9. **Ask participants to sketch** during journey mapping (increases engagement)
10. **Work with real users** whenever possible (nothing beats actual customer voice)

## Final Thoughts

Service blueprint workshops are about **alignment and action**, not perfect documentation.

"The map is not the territory, and the territory is constantly changing. Focus on creating shared understanding that drives measurable improvements." - Paraphrasing Stickdorn

Success measures:
- ✅ Cross-functional team aligned on customer experience
- ✅ Top 3 pain points identified with evidence
- ✅ Top 2-3 opportunities committed to roadmap
- ✅ Blueprint becomes reference artifact for next 6 months
- ✅ Team excited about improvements and next steps

**Not** success measures:
- ❌ Perfect blueprint with every detail
- ❌ Everyone agrees on everything
- ❌ Beautiful visual with no follow-through
- ❌ Document stored and never referenced again
