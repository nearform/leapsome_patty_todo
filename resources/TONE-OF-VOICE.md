# Nearform Tone of Voice & Writing Guidelines

*Note: Always use "Nearform" (not "NearForm") and write in UK English (e.g., "realise," "colour," "optimise").*

## Our Voice

Nearform speaks with the confidence of a team that's built the technologies others depend on. We're the engineers who contributed 30% of Node.js v10 globally, but we don't need to remind you of that in every conversation. Our expertise shows through clarity, not complexity.

We sound like the colleague you'd want on your toughest technical challenge — knowledgeable but not condescending, direct but not dismissive. When we explain complex systems, we do it because we want you to succeed, not because we want to impress you.

## Writing Principles

IMPORTANT: 
- Always refer to Nearform not NearForm
- Keep condensed formatting, do not add an empty space after a heading or bold heading.

### 1. Write Human
Technology serves people. Our writing should too.

**The human touch means:**
- Using "we" and "you" instead of hiding behind corporate speak
- Sharing insights, not just information
- Acknowledging when things are complex (because they often are)
- Adding personality through natural language choices

**Instead of:** "The implementation of our proprietary methodologies ensures optimal outcomes for enterprise-level digital transformation initiatives."

**Try:** "We've developed an approach that helps large companies modernize their systems without the usual headaches. It works because we've learned from every project what actually matters."

### 2. Cut Through the Noise
If you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it well enough. Our clients don't have time for fluff.

**Avoid Latin and foreign expressions:**
- "e.g." → "for example"
- "i.e." → "that is"
- "etc." → "and so on"
- Exception: "vice versa" saves many words of explanation

**Noise reduction checklist:**
- Replace unnecessarily long phrases:
  - "in order to" → "to"
  - "due to the fact that" → "because"
  - "with regards to" → "about"
  - "until such time as" → "until"

- Eliminate redundant adjectives:
  - "end result" → "result"
  - "past history" → "history"
  - "new initiative" → "initiative"
  - "basic essentials" → "essentials"

- Use active voice:
  - "It is recommended that teams should" → "Teams should"
  - "There has been a decline in performance" → "Performance declined"

**Avoid weak clichés and jargon:**
- "Slimming down" (processes don't diet)
- "Foster" (unless it's children)
- "Agenda" (unless it's for a meeting)
- "Deliver" (pizzas and services are delivered, not abstract concepts like "improvements")
- "Deploy" (unless it's military or software)
- "Key" (unless it unlocks something - use "important" instead)
- "Going forward" (we're not giving travel directions)

**Be consistent with word choice:**
Use the same term for the same concept throughout your document, especially for non-native English speakers.

### 3. Respect Intelligence, Enable Understanding
Our audience is smart. They don't need us to oversimplify, but they do need us to be clear.

**Balance complexity with clarity:**
- Use technical terms when they're the most precise choice
- Define specialized concepts without talking down
- Provide context for why something matters
- Connect technical details to business outcomes

## Content Types & Approaches

### LinkedIn & Thought Leadership
- **Start with insight, not introduction:** Jump into the interesting part
- **Use data to challenge assumptions:** "Everyone talks about digital transformation. Here's what actually works."
- **Share specific examples:** Replace "Many companies struggle with legacy systems" with "We helped a retailer reduce their content update time from 24 hours to 5 minutes"
- **End with a question or call to action:** Drive engagement, don't just broadcast

### Client Communications
- **Lead with outcomes:** What will this mean for their business?
- **Acknowledge complexity:** "This integration involves several moving parts, but here's how we'll manage the risks..."
- **Provide clear next steps:** Always end with what happens next
- **Use project-specific context:** Reference their goals and constraints

### Technical Documentation
- **Structure for scanning:** Use headings, bullets, and visual breaks
- **Start with the "why":** Context before instructions
- **Provide examples:** Show, don't just tell
- **Test for comprehension:** Can someone follow these steps without asking questions?

## Writing Approach
When reviewing your writing, ask: "Does this sound like something a thoughtful expert would say to a colleague?" Then add:

**Natural quirks:**
- Mild personal observations: "In our experience..." or "We've noticed that..."
- Casual asides: "This is particularly important for teams using microservices (and who isn't these days?)"
- Honest uncertainty: "This approach works well for most cases, though every situation has its nuances"

**Varied sentence structure:**
- Mix short, punchy statements with longer explanatory sentences
- Use fragments occasionally for emphasis. Like this.
- Ask rhetorical questions when they help make a point

**Human imperfections:**
- Contractions where they feel natural
- Slight inconsistencies that show personality
- Opinions backed by experience

## Grammar Reality Check
Ignore these outdated "rules":

1. **Starting sentences with conjunctions** - Go ahead. But make sure the sentence is complete.
2. **Comma before "and"** - Use one if it helps clarity
3. **Ending with prepositions** - "This is the system we want to invest in" sounds better than awkward alternatives
4. **Split infinitives** - "We need to properly configure the system" is clearer than "We need properly to configure the system"
5. **One-sentence paragraphs** - Sometimes they work perfectly.

## Visual Presentation Guidelines

### Text Structure
- **Sentence length:** Average 15-20 words per sentence, mixing shorter and longer for variety
- **Text density:** Include adequate white space between lines and paragraphs
- **Sub-headings:** Break up long passages to help readers scan and find information
- **Lists:** Use bulleted and numbered lists to organize information clearly

### Visual Hierarchy
- **Headline hierarchy:** Use size to differentiate primary and secondary headings
- **Z-pattern layout:** Place important elements along the natural reading path

## Voice Checklist

**Sounds human:**
- [ ] Uses "we" and "you" appropriately
- [ ] Includes personal pronouns and active voice
- [ ] Shows personality through word choice
- [ ] Acknowledges when things are genuinely complex

**Cuts through noise:**
- [ ] Uses simple words where possible
- [ ] Eliminates unnecessary phrases and qualifiers
- [ ] Replaces bureaucratic language with direct communication
- [ ] Makes every sentence earn its place

**Respects the reader:**
- [ ] Starts with insights, not introductions
- [ ] Provides context for technical concepts
- [ ] Connects features to outcomes
- [ ] Ends with clear next steps or engaging questions

**Feels authentic:**
- [ ] Reflects genuine expertise without showing off
- [ ] Uses contractions and natural language patterns
- [ ] Varies sentence length and structure

**Looks professional:**
- [ ] Uses clear, readable typography and appropriate sizing
- [ ] Includes adequate white space and visual breaks
- [ ] Organizes information with headings and lists
- [ ] Maintains consistent visual hierarchy and color usage

## Examples in Practice

### Before: Corporate Speak
"Nearform's comprehensive suite of digital transformation solutions leverages cutting-edge methodologies to optimize enterprise-level technology implementations, ensuring optimal outcomes through our proven track record of successful deliverables."

### After: Human Expert
"We help companies modernize their technology systems without the usual chaos. Our approach works because we've been building the tools others depend on — and we've learned what actually matters from every project."

### Before: Overly Technical
"Our proprietary SDLC optimization framework utilizes advanced CI/CD pipeline configurations with automated testing protocols to minimize deployment risks while maximizing operational efficiency."

### After: Technical but Clear
"We've developed a way to help teams ship code faster and more safely. It combines smart automation with the testing approaches that catch problems before they reach customers — something that's especially valuable when you're modernizing complex systems."
